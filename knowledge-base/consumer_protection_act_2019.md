# Consumer Protection Act 2019

## Overview
The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 is a comprehensive legislation enacted to protect consumer rights and provide effective mechanisms for resolution of consumer disputes. It supersedes the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 and incorporates modern provisions for protection of consumers.

## Part I: Preliminary

### Section 1 - Short Title, Extent and Commencement
- **Short Title**: Consumer Protection Act, 2019
- **Extent**: Extends to the whole of India except Jammu and Kashmir
- **Commencement**: July 20, 2020 (Full implementation)

### Section 2 - Definitions

#### Key Definitions:
- **"Advertisement"**: Any form of representation made by a trader to promote goods or services
- **"Complaint"**: Any grievance regarding a trade practice or violation of consumer rights
- **"Consumer"**: Any natural person who purchases goods or services for personal use
- **"Defect"**: Goods not conforming to agreed specification or any hidden defect
- **"Deficiency"**: Service not provided with due care, skill and timely manner
- **"Goods"**: Every kind of moveable property
- **"Manufacturer"**: Person engaged in the business of making goods for sale
- **"Service provider"**: Person who offers or renders any service
- **"Trader"**: Any person who offers or renders goods or services for consideration

## Part II: Consumer Rights and Responsibilities

### Section 6 - Consumer Rights

Every consumer has the right to:

#### 1. Right to Safety (Section 6(a))
- Protection against goods and services that are hazardous
- Right to information about potential dangers
- Right to demand safe products

#### 2. Right to Information (Section 6(b))
- Information about quality, quantity, potency
- Purity and standard of goods or services
- Price of goods or services
- Presence of any additives or harmful substances
- Expiry date and manufacturing date
- Any other information required to protect consumer interests

#### 3. Right to Choose (Section 6(c))
- Assurance that consumer will be assured of competitive prices
- Right to choose from available options
- Right not to be coerced into purchasing unwanted products

#### 4. Right to be Heard (Section 6(d))
- Right to file complaint before consumer commission
- Right to be heard in any consumer proceeding
- Right to present views and evidence

#### 5. Right to Seek Redressal (Section 6(e))
- Right to recover damages in case of loss or injury
- Right to demand compensation
- Right to file complaint for unfair trade practices

#### 6. Right to Consumer Education (Section 6(f))
- Right to be informed about consumer rights and responsibilities
- Right to access information about products and services
- Right to guidance on rational consumption

### Section 7 - Consumer Responsibilities
Every consumer shall:
- Be aware of consumer rights and responsibilities
- Exercise rights in good faith
- Seek redressal within prescribed periods
- Not engage in unfair trade practices
- Cooperate with authorities in consumer protection

## Part III: Protection of Consumer Rights

### Section 8 - Unfair Trade Practices
The following are considered unfair trade practices:

#### (1) Goods and Services Unfair Trade Practices:
- Representation of goods/services as per specifications when they are not
- Offering goods/services having hidden defects
- Making false or misleading representation
- Offering false warranty or guarantee
- Issuing misleading prospectuses or advertisements
- Using false claim in advertisement about goods/services
- Offer of free samples with intention to charge later
- Undertaking corrective advertising
- Creating false belief about monopoly position
- Inducing customer to purchase through false or misleading information
- False representation about price or value

#### (2) Specific Unfair Practices:
- Advertising hazardous goods
- Offering gifts/prizes fraudulently
- Sponsoring false testimonials
- Making false claims about any expert approval
- Supply of spurious goods
- Exploitation of consumer's ignorance

### Section 9 - Deceptive Advertisements

#### (1) Prohibition
No trader shall publish any advertisement:
- Which falsely represents goods or services
- Which contains false claims about benefits or advantages
- Which makes claims not substantiated by reliable data or evidence
- Which exploits consumer superstition or fear

#### (2) Punishment for Deceptive Advertisement
First conviction: Imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to ₹10 lakhs
Subsequent conviction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to ₹50 lakhs

### Section 10 - False or Misleading Advertisements About Drugs
Special provisions for misleading advertisement of drugs and medicines:
- Cannot claim cure or complete relief from disease
- Cannot recommend or suggest drug can replace medical treatment
- Cannot encourage self-medication

## Part IV: Product Liability

### Section 2 - Definition of Product Liability
Seller or manufacturer is liable for:
- Damage caused by defective goods
- Loss or injury due to deficiency in services
- Death or injury caused by hazardous products

### Section 96 - Recovery of Damages
Consumer can recover damages for:
- Economic loss
- Personal injury or death
- Psychological impact
- Loss of business opportunity

## Part V: Consumer Dispute Redressal

### Section 12 - Consumer Commissions

#### District Consumer Commission:
- For consumer complaints where value of goods/services and compensation claimed does not exceed ₹1 crore

#### State Consumer Commission:
- For consumer complaints where value exceeds ₹1 crore but does not exceed ₹10 crores

#### National Consumer Commission:
- For consumer complaints where value exceeds ₹10 crores
- For matters of public importance

### Section 2(c) - Consumer Complaint Defined
A consumer complaint includes:
- Any goods sold in violation of Consumer Protection Act provisions
- Any service rendered in violation of statutory obligations
- False or misleading advertisement
- Unfair or restrictive trade practice
- Demand for unfair or unreasonable value

### Section 35 - Filing Consumer Complaint

#### Section 35(1) - Eligibility to File
A consumer can file complaint for:
- Defect in goods
- Deficiency in service
- Unfair or restrictive trade practice
- False or misleading advertisement
- Violation of any provisions of the Act

#### Section 35(2) - Subject Matter
Complaint shall relate to:
- Loss or injury to consumer
- Unfair trade practice
- Violation of consumer rights
- Non-compliance with standards

#### Section 35(3) - Limitation Period
Complaint must be filed:
- **Within 2 years from date of defect/deficiency discovery**
- Or from date when consumer reasonably should have discovered it
- Maximum 5 years from date of transaction

#### Section 35(4) - Complaint Procedure
Consumer can file complaint:
- In writing with all particulars
- In person or through authorized representative
- By post, email, or any other mode prescribed
- In any language with English/Hindi translation

### Section 36 - General Remedy

#### Section 36(1) - Remedies Available
The Commission can:
- Declare goods as defective
- Declare service as deficient
- Award compensation for loss or injury
- Direct removal of goods from the market
- Direct cease of misleading advertisement
- Direct refund or replacement
- Award compensation for mental suffering and loss of time
- Direct action against trader/manufacturer
- Impose fine for non-compliance

#### Section 36(2) - Compensation for Non-Complying Traders
- Fine up to ₹10 lakhs for non-compliance with order
- Imprisonment up to 1 year or both

### Section 2(g) - Definition of Defect in Goods
Goods shall be deemed to have defect if:
- They do not conform to agreed specification
- They have become defective due to manufacturing defect
- They contain hidden defects reducing utility
- They are unfit for intended purpose
- They lack essential characteristics

### Section 2(h) - Definition of Deficiency in Service
Service shall be deemed deficient if:
- Not rendered in agreed manner and within time
- Not rendered with due care, skill and diligence
- Not provided as per statutory requirements
- Contains hidden defects
- Cannot be used for intended purpose

## Part VI: Penalties

### Section 92 - Penalty for Violation
For violation of provisions:
- First violation: Fine up to ₹10 lakhs
- Subsequent violation: Fine up to ₹50 lakhs

### Section 93 - Imprisonment for Wilful Violation
For willful violation:
- First offence: Imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to ₹10 lakhs
- Second offence: Imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to ₹50 lakhs

## Procedure for Filing Consumer Complaint

### Step 1: Identify the Forum
- **District Commission**: ₹1 crore and below
- **State Commission**: ₹1-10 crores
- **National Commission**: Above ₹10 crores

### Step 2: Prepare Complaint
- Detailed description of defect/deficiency
- Amount of compensation claimed
- Documents supporting the claim
- Date of purchase/transaction
- Evidence of quality standards

### Step 3: File Complaint
- Pay required fee (₹0-₹5000 depending on claim value)
- Submit in prescribed format
- Include copies of supporting documents
- Keep original documents for hearing

### Step 4: Commission Review
- Commission will examine complaint
- Can seek clarifications from both parties
- May conduct inspection or testing if required

### Step 5: Hearing and Order
- Both parties get opportunity to present case
- Commission will deliver order with reasoning
- Order is binding on both parties
- Non-compliance can attract penalties

## Common Consumer Grievances and Remedies

### Defective Products
- **Remedy**: Refund, replacement, or compensation
- **Evidence Required**: Proof of purchase, photos of defect, warranty documents

### Non-Delivery of Goods
- **Remedy**: Refund with interest
- **Timeline**: File complaint within 6 months of transaction

### Misleading Advertisement
- **Remedy**: Compensation for financial loss and mental suffering
- **Action**: Commission may impose fine on advertiser

### Poor Service Quality
- **Remedy**: Refund of service charges, compensation for damages
- **Example**: Poor electrical repair, incomplete painting job

### Hidden Defects
- **Remedy**: Replacement or refund
- **Period**: Can file within 2 years of discovery

## E-Commerce Consumer Rights

### Additional Protections for Online Purchases:
- Right to return goods within 30 days
- Right to refund without charges
- Right to information about seller
- Protection against unfair trade practices
- Right to dispute fraudulent transactions

## Important Appeals Framework

- **First Appeal**: To District/State/National Commission within 30 days
- **Second Appeal**: To National Commission from District Commission within 30 days
- **Third Appeal**: To Supreme Court on questions of law
