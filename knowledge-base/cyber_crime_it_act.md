# Information Technology Act 2000 & Cyber Crime Provisions

## Part I: Overview

### Introduction
The Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000 (as amended by IT Amendment Act, 2008) is the primary legislation in India addressing cybercrimes, electronic documents, and digital signatures. It is complemented by the Indian Penal Code (IPC) which contains provisions for cyber-enabled crimes.

### Jurisdiction and Scope
- Applies to whole of India
- Also applies to cyber crimes targeting Indian citizens/systems from outside India
- Special cyber crime units established at Central, State, and District levels

## Part II: Cyber Crimes under IT Act 2000

### Section 66 - Computer Related Offences

#### Section 66(1) - Unauthorized Access
**Offence**: A person who:
- Accesses a computer, computer system, or computer network without authorization
- With intent to cause wrongful loss or gain
- Or knowing it will cause loss or damage

**Punishment**: 
- Imprisonment up to 3 years
- Fine up to ₹1 lakh
- Or both

#### Section 66(2) - Unauthorized Access Causing Damage
**Offence**: Access causing damage valued at ₹5 lakhs or more

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 10 years
- Fine up to ₹2 lakhs
- Or both

### Section 66B - Computer Fraud
**Offence**: Whoever dishonestly or fraudulently:
- Introduces computer virus into computer system
- Destroys data with intent to cause loss
- Fraudulently obtains computer resource or data

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 3 years
- Fine up to ₹1 lakh
- Or both

### Section 66C - Identity Theft
**Offence**: Whoever:
- Fraudulently or dishonestly uses/transmits passwords or secret codes
- Gains unauthorized access to computer system
- With intention to commit fraud or cause wrongful loss

**Examples**:
- Using someone's credentials without permission
- Stealing OTP/passwords to access accounts
- Creating fake profiles to commit fraud

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 3 years
- Fine up to ₹1 lakh
- Or both

### Section 66D - Phishing and Social Engineering
**Offence**: Whoever:
- Induces person to disclose personal information through false representation
- Uses deceptive means to obtain sensitive data
- Commits cheating through email, SMS, or digital communication

**Examples**:
- Fake bank emails requesting account details
- SMS pretending to be from legitimate organization
- Posing as customer service to extract credentials

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 3 years
- Fine up to ₹1 lakh
- Or both

### Section 66E - Violation of Privacy
**Offence**: Whoever:
- Transmits obscene material in electronic form
- Captures images of person without consent
- Publishes private images without permission
- Violates privacy intentionally

**Examples**:
- Non-consensual intimate image sharing
- Recording private conversation without permission
- Distributing private photos without consent

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 3 years
- Fine up to ₹2 lakhs
- Or both

### Section 66F - Cyber Terrorism
**Offence**: Whoever:
- Attacks computer system with intent to threaten national security
- Causes loss of life or damage to critical infrastructure
- Creates terror through cyber means
- Threatens government functioning

**Examples**:
- Hacking government/defense systems
- Attacking critical infrastructure (power grids, water systems)
- Threatening government through cyber means
- DDoS attacks on national security systems

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to life imprisonment
- Fine up to ₹10 crores
- Or both

## Part III: Cyber Crimes under Indian Penal Code (IPC)

### Section 419 IPC - Cheating by Impersonation
**Applicable to Cyber Domain**: 
- Posing as someone else online to cheat
- Creating fake profiles to commit fraud

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 1 year
- Fine up to ₹1,000
- Or both

### Section 420 IPC - Cheating and Dishonestly Inducing Delivery of Property
**Applicable Cyber Context**:
- Cyber fraud inducing person to transfer money/property
- Online scams promising false returns
- Investment fraud through online platforms

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 7 years
- Fine up to ₹unlimited
- Or both

### Section 468 IPC - Forgery for Cheating
**Applicable Cyber Context**:
- Creating forged digital documents
- Falsifying electronic records
- Creating fake certificates/documentation

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 7 years
- Fine up to ₹unlimited
- Or both

### Section 469 IPC - Forgery Knowing it Will Be Used for Cheating
**Applicable Cyber Context**:
- Creating false digital evidence with intent to use for fraud
- Fabricating electronic records for cheating

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 7 years
- Fine up to ₹unlimited
- Or both

### Section 509 IPC - Word, Gesture, Act Intended to Insult Modesty
**Applicable Cyber Context**:
- Cyber harassment
- Sending obscene messages/images
- Online bullying with sexual overtones

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 3 years
- Fine up to ₹unlimited
- Or both

### Section 507 IPC - Criminal Intimidation by Anonymous Communication
**Applicable Cyber Context**:
- Anonymous threatening emails/messages
- Cyber threats and blackmail
- Threatening via social media

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to 2 years
- Fine up to ₹unlimited
- Or both

## Part IV: Specific Cyber Crime Categories

### Online Banking Fraud

#### Types:
- Phishing emails from fake bank domains
- Fake banking apps installed on victims' phones
- OTP theft through social engineering
- Man-in-the-middle attacks

#### Investigation Approach:
- File complaint with bank immediately
- Block compromised accounts
- Collect evidence of fake communications
- File police complaint with copies
- Contact RBI if systemic issue

#### Available Remedies:
- Bank account freeze/recovery
- Fund transfer reversal (if within 48 hours)
- Fraud compensation from bank
- Police action against perpetrators

### E-Commerce Fraud

#### Types:
- Fake seller listing non-existent goods
- Charging for goods not delivered
- Receiving substandard products
- Unauthorized transactions

#### Grievance Resolution:
- Report to e-commerce platform
- File dispute with payment processor
- Consumer complaint to District Commission
- Police complaint for serious fraud

### Investment/Trading Fraud

#### Types:
- Fake investment schemes promising guaranteed returns
- Pump and dump schemes
- Binary options/forex scams
- Cryptocurrency fraud

#### Regulatory Bodies:
- SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India)
- RBI (Reserve Bank of India)
- IRDA (Insurance Regulatory Development Authority)

### Romance/Dating Fraud

#### Types:
- Catfishing for financial exploitation
- Fake profile romance scams
- Advance fee fraud

#### Investigation:
- Document all communications
- Report to dating platform
- File cyber crime complaint
- Contact CBI for large amounts

### Ransomware Attacks

#### Types:
- Malware encrypting personal files
- Demanding payment for decryption
- Threatening to publish sensitive data

#### Response:
- Do NOT pay ransom (encourages more attacks)
- Backup systems if possible
- File police complaint
- Contact cybersecurity agencies
- Inform CERT-IN

## Part V: Complaint and Investigation Process

### Section 67 - Penalty for Publishing Obscene Material
**Punishment**:
- First offence: Imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to ₹10 lakhs
- Subsequent offence: Imprisonment up to 7 years and fine up to ₹10 lakhs

### Cyber Crime Reporting Procedures

#### Step 1: Immediate Actions
- Preserve all evidence (screenshots, emails, messages)
- Document all communications with fraudster
- Note dates, times, and amounts involved
- Change passwords and secure accounts
- Report to relevant organization (bank, platform, etc.)

#### Step 2: File FIR (First Information Report)
- Go to nearest Cyber Police Unit or Police Station
- File FIR with complete details
- Provide all evidence and documentation
- Get FIR copy with reference number
- Police will initiate investigation

#### Step 3: File Complaint with Specialized Agencies
- **CERT-IN** (Cyber Emergency Response Team India): For infrastructure attacks
- **CBI**: For major fraud cases (amount > ₹1 crore)
- **Economic Offences Wing**: For financial crimes
- **Cybercrime portal** (cybercrime.gov.in): For online complaint

#### Step 4: Civil/Consumer Remedy
- File consumer complaint if e-commerce involved
- File civil suit for recovery
- Seek compensation for damages

### Jurisdiction for Cyber Crimes

#### Police Jurisdiction:
- Crime committed in police station area
- Crime affecting residents of that area
- Crime committed using computer system located there

#### Multi-State Cyber Crimes:
- Investigated by CBI if:
  - Amount involved exceeds ₹1 crore
  - Multiple states/countries involved
  - National security implications

## Part VI: Bank and Payment Protection

### RBI Guidelines for Online Fraud

#### Bank's Responsibility:
- Reimburse unauthorized transactions within 24-48 hours
- Cooperate with police investigation
- Implement security measures
- Issue new cards/accounts immediately

#### Complainant's Rights:
- Free reimbursement for unauthorized transactions
- No questioning of legitimacy of complaint initially
- Bank must reverse charges while investigating
- Compensation for mental suffering

### Payment Aggregator Liability
- Payment gateways liable for fraudulent transactions
- Must reverse unauthorized payments
- Must provide transaction logs to police
- Must implement 3D secure and OTP verification

## Investigation Timeline

### Expected Investigation Phases:
1. **FIR Registration**: Same day
2. **Initial Investigation**: 7-15 days
3. **Evidence Collection**: 15-30 days
4. **Bank Account Freezing**: 5-10 days
5. **Chargesheet Filing**: 90-180 days
6. **First Arrest Possible**: 60-120 days

## Important Contacts

### National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal
- **Website**: cybercrime.gov.in
- **Purpose**: Register cyber crime complaints
- **Available**: 24/7

### CERT-IN (Cyber Emergency Response Team)
- **Email**: incident@cert.gov.in
- **Purpose**: Report cybersecurity threats to infrastructure
- **Emergency Hotline**: Available

### Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)
- **Website**: cbi.gov.in
- **Jurisdiction**: Cases exceeding ₹1 crore
- **Cyber Crime Unit**: Dedicated division

### Cybercrime Police Units
- Available in most state capitals
- Equipped with digital forensics labs
- Trained cyber crime investigators
